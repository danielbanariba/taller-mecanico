# Honduran and Central American local vendors of POS / inventory / invoicing software for small businesses, with emphasis on talleres mecánicos and repuesteras (auto parts stores)

## ¿Qué empresas hondureñas o centroamericanas venden software de POS/inventario/facturación a pequeños negocios, y específicamente a talleres mecánicos o repuesteras?

### Takeaway
The web presence of Honduras-specific POS/inventory/invoicing software is very thin. Across roughly a dozen targeted searches in Spanish and English, only one candidate product (Zafra Cloud) explicitly claims Honduras/SAR certification and SMB targeting, and even that product does **not** mention talleres mecánicos or repuesteras anywhere in its listing. The only other vendor with a confirmed, named Honduran presence is Softland (a Central/South American regional ERP vendor with a Honduras site and at least one named Honduran client), which is a general-purpose ERP, not an automotive-shop-specific product. No vendor found explicitly advertises to "talleres mecánicos" or "repuesteras" as a niche.

### Cited Findings
- **Zafra Cloud** is described as a "100% cloud-based management system" for SMBs, explicitly targeting "Honduras and Central America," offering invoicing, point-of-sale, multi-warehouse inventory, automated accounting, and CRM. Sectors explicitly listed are retail stores, restaurants, distributors, clinics, and professional services — auto repair shops or auto parts stores are **not** mentioned — [Source](https://mwm.ai/apps/zafra-cloud/6771561083)
- The Zafra Cloud app listing shows a release date of July 12, 2026, and lists the publisher as "Isabella Madrid" (an individual-sounding name rather than a registered company name as commonly seen on App Store listings) — [Source](https://mwm.ai/apps/zafra-cloud/6771561083)
- **Softland** (a regional ERP vendor headquartered outside Honduras, with Chilean/Central American operations) maintains a Honduras-specific site (softland.com/hn) and offers a "Colnet – Elisium" point-of-sale module as part of its commercial-area ERP suite, aimed at retail and food/beverage businesses, integrated in real time with the core ERP — [Source](https://softland.com/hn/?p=1337)
- Softland's Chile-hosted marketing materials include a Honduras case study titled "Banasupro HN," referring to Banasupro (Banco Nacional de Suministros / Honduran government-subsidized basic-goods retail chain), indicating Softland has at least one large, named Honduran retail client — [Source](https://preview.softland.cl/cl/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Banasupro-HN-1.pdf)

### Inferences

## ¿Cómo cumplen con el régimen de facturación del SAR hondureño (CAI, autoimpresor, facturación electrónica) estos productos?

### Takeaway
The Honduran invoicing regime itself is well documented by tax-compliance trackers: it centers on the CAI (Código/Clave de Autorización de Impresión) pre-authorization model administered by SAR, with two valid issuance paths — "imprenta" (SAR-certified print shops) and "autoimpresor" (self-printing from SAR-registered software) — plus an optional, non-mandatory electronic-invoicing modality (CAEE, Acuerdo 481-2017). Of the actual software products found, only Zafra Cloud makes an explicit SAR-Honduras compliance claim; no official SAR-published list of certified autoimpresor software providers was located.

### Cited Findings
- Every invoice issued in Honduras must carry a CAI, a unique authorization code SAR assigns to each batch of invoices before they can be printed or generated, under a pre-authorization-of-numbering model — [Source](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/honduras-invoice-requirements)
- Invoices reach the market through one of two modalities: "autoimpresor" (businesses self-print from SAR-registered systems) or "imprenta" (SAR-certified printing companies produce the documents); taxpayers issue documents within an authorized numeric range without real-time SAR validation of each individual document — [Source](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/honduras-invoice-requirements)
- Valid Honduran invoices require: a 14-digit taxpayer RTN (Registro Tributario Nacional), a 16-digit sequential invoice number, a line-item ISV tax breakdown at the standard 15% rate, and the CAI's expiration date; CAI authorizations have a maximum validity of one year, and documents issued outside that period/range are invalid for tax purposes — [Source](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/honduras-invoice-requirements)
- Acuerdo 481-2017 allows an optional "Facturación Electrónica" (CAEE) self-printing modality for electronic creation and preservation of fiscal documents; this is explicitly **not** the same as a fully SAR-cleared, mandatory e-invoicing (e-CF) system and is not compulsory — [Source](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/08/11/honduras-e-invoicing-e-reporting-country-booklet/)
- As of the booklet dated 2026-08-11, a mandatory nationwide Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) clearance e-invoicing system is being introduced by SAR in phases, with taxpayers in scope required to generate XML invoices, digitally sign them, and submit them to SAR for validation before issuing them to customers — [Source](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/08/11/honduras-e-invoicing-e-reporting-country-booklet/)
- La Prensa (a major Honduran newspaper) published an article headlined "El SAR crea acceso en línea para impresión de facturas" ("SAR creates online access for invoice printing"), indicating SAR has built or is building an online portal relevant to invoice-printing authorization; full article content was not retrieved in this research session, so only the headline fact is cited — [Source](https://www.laprensa.hn/honduras/el-sar-crea-acceso-en-linea-para-impresion-de-facturas-PSLP972508)
- Zafra Cloud's product listing explicitly claims to be "officially certified by SAR Honduras," stating this "ensures that the platform complies with local fiscal regulations and requirements" — marked explicitly as a vendor claim, not independently verified — [Source](https://mwm.ai/apps/zafra-cloud/6771561083)
- No other product found in this research (Softland, Odoo, or any other candidate) made an explicit, citable SAR/CAI/autoimpresor compliance claim in the material retrieved.

### Inferences
- The CAI/autoimpresor regime (not a real-time e-invoicing clearance system for most taxpayers, per the sources above) is less technically demanding than, e.g., Costa Rica's or Mexico's electronic invoicing, which likely lowers the barrier for any software vendor — local or foreign — to claim "compliance," since a self-printer only needs to respect the assigned numeric range and required fields, not integrate with a live government API. This makes vendor compliance claims easier to make but harder to verify independently without checking the actual CAI registration.
- The fact that a mandatory CTC-style e-invoicing rollout is underway (per the 2026-08-11 vatupdate booklet) means any vendor serving Honduran talleres/repuesteras today should be evaluated not just on current CAI/autoimpresor support but on its roadmap for the incoming mandatory e-invoicing phases — none of the vendors found in this research disclosed such a roadmap.

### Gaps
- No official SAR-published list of certified "autoimpresor" software providers or authorized printing houses ("imprentas") was found via general web search (queries for "lista autoimpresores certificados SAR Honduras" returned no direct SAR directory page). A direct crawl of sar.gob.hn (e.g., via site:sar.gob.hn) was not attempted due to tool/time constraints in this session — this is a concrete follow-up research action for whoever continues this work.
- Zafra Cloud's "officially certified by SAR Honduras" claim could not be independently corroborated against any SAR source or third-party registry.
- The exact scope, timeline, and which taxpayer categories are currently subject to the mandatory CTC e-invoicing phase-in (relevant to small talleres/repuesteras specifically) was not resolved in detail — the source found describes the general mechanism but not a firm itemized phase-in calendar by taxpayer size.

### Inferences
- The absence of reviews is consistent with the broader finding (Key Question 1) that this market segment has very little independent web indexing — it likely exists primarily on Facebook (a platform general web search does not deeply index for business-page comments/reviews) and through in-person/WhatsApp relationships, which are invisible to this research method.
- If this project's own software is positioned against local competitors, the lack of any critical public feedback on them means claims about competitor weaknesses cannot be sourced from user reviews for this market — any competitive positioning must rely on feature/price comparison or direct interviews with taller/repuestera owners, not desk research.

### Gaps
- No Facebook or Google review data could be obtained for any Honduran vendor; this requires either direct Facebook/Google Maps search access (not available via the tools used in this session) or primary interviews with Honduran taller/repuestera owners, which is outside the scope of this desk-research task.
- It is unknown whether this absence reflects genuinely low review volume (plausible for a small, relationship-based B2B market) or simply a search-indexing limitation — this distinction could not be resolved with the tools available.

## Key Learnings
1. General web search (non-Facebook) surfaces almost no independently reviewable content for Honduran B2B software microvendors, confirming Facebook-native search or direct outreach is required to assess this market segment.
2. Searching generic terms like "software para repuestos Honduras" returns false positives from other countries (e.g., a Mexican GNcys product referencing "SAT" rather than Honduras's "SAR"), so compliance-terminology mismatches are a reliable tell for filtering out non-Honduran results.
3. Honduras-focused academic repositories (UNITEC) are a useful secondary source for market-gap evidence (e.g., Mypyme software adoption rates) even when they do not name commercial vendors directly.
4. The only product found explicitly claiming Honduras SAR certification (Zafra Cloud) was released in July 2026 and has no independent reviews, so it should be treated as an unverified, early-stage claim rather than an established market player.
